## 4. Kim’s Perspective

### Case Origin
- Professor at Coast Guard Academy.
- Initial issue: harassment and bullying by superior.
- Attempted informal resolution; failed.
- Escalated to formal complaints.

### Institutional Response
- Chain of command repeatedly covered up findings.
- Investigations misrepresented or suppressed.
- Experienced retaliation and health decline.
- Issues included broader systemic misconduct.

### Inspector General Path
- Independently discovered IG complaint option.
- Not clearly documented in official materials.
- Filed successfully; first known case of its kind in her context.
- Triggered broader wave of complaints.

### Congressional Involvement
- Parallel investigation by Congress.
- Evidence gathered across multiple years.
- Findings aligned with IG conclusions.

### Structural Barriers
- Military members generally cannot sue government.
- Law firms reluctant due to lack of financial incentive.
- Paid legal costs out-of-pocket.
- No meaningful accountability despite findings.

### Ongoing Impact
- Continues to face public and institutional targeting.
- Linked to political and institutional dynamics.
- Views this as part of broader suppression of whistleblowing.

### Definition of Success
- Personal integrity and ability to “sleep at night.”
- Preserving dignity and agency.
- Educating others about rights and systems.
- Not dependent on legal accountability outcomes.

## 5. What Defines “Success” for Whistleblowers?

### Erika
- Success is context-dependent.
- Core goal: stopping harmful behavior.
- Layers of success:
  - Immediate harm reduction
  - Preventing recurrence
  - Systemic change
  - Justice/accountability (often out of control)
- Avoid tying identity to outcomes beyond control.

### Kim
- Initially focused on stopping misconduct.
- Shifted to preserving dignity and identity.
- Success = maintaining self-worth and educating others.
- Does not expect full accountability within lifetime.

### Glenn
- Noted multiple definitions of success.
- Highlighted risk of identity becoming consumed by whistleblowing.

## 6. Hush Line Product Discussion (First-Time Experience)

### Current Features (as described by Glenn)
- Safety guidance:
  - Do not use work devices/networks.
  - Consult lawyer before collecting evidence.
  - Media disclosure risks legal protections.
- No account required to submit information.
- Legal disclaimer (not legal advice).
- Exit mechanism to neutral site (Wikipedia).
- Optional Tor usage guidance.

### Erika’s Feedback
- Strong onboarding overall.
- Suggest adding mental health acknowledgment (carefully worded).
- Avoid adding too much information to first screen.
- Suggest deeper resources elsewhere in product.

### Kim’s Feedback
- Noted readability issues on mobile.
- Suggested “expectation-setting” tools:
  - Likelihood of success
  - Common outcomes
  - Typical risks
- Emphasized importance of informed decision-making.

### Glenn’s Ideas
- “Concierge” concept:
  - Match users with lawyers, journalists, support resources.
- Interest in tools showing:
  - historical outcomes
  - case expectations
  - recommended pathways

### Erika’s Caution
- Avoid discouraging users with overly negative statistics.
- Balance realism with examples of meaningful success.
- Emphasize strategy over impulsive action.
